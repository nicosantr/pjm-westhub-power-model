# PJM Western Hub Power Price Model

How much do weather and natural gas move PJM Western Hub on-peak power prices, and how well do those two drivers predict the next day's price?

Power traders describe PJM prices as mostly a function of **weather** (which drives load) and **fuel** (gas sets the marginal price in most hours). This project measures that relationship and turns it into a daily fair-value estimate for Western Hub, PJM's most liquid trading point.

## Results (2026 out-of-sample)

Trained on 2023 to 2025, tested day by day on 225 on-peak days in 2026. Errors in $/MWh.

| Model | MAE | RMSE | MAE winter | MAE rest |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **SARIMAX(1,0,1) + weather/gas** | **24.29** | 53.40 | 31.79 | 22.62 |
| Heat-rate model x gas | 26.03 | **50.49** | **24.72** | 26.32 |
| Regression: + squared weather + lag | 26.87 | 59.07 | 36.66 | 24.69 |
| Naive: previous day (benchmark) | 33.39 | 69.75 | 55.90 | 28.38 |
| Exponential smoothing (alpha=0.95) | 33.87 | 70.51 | 56.95 | 28.72 |
| Naive: same day last year | 50.29 | 106.81 | 105.29 | 38.03 |

Full 15-model table: `model_comparison.csv` (generated by the notebook).

- The best weather/gas model cut average error **27%** vs. the naive previous-day benchmark ($24.29 vs. $33.39/MWh).
- The gas-scaled **heat-rate model cut winter error 56%** ($24.72 vs. $55.90/MWh) and had the lowest RMSE, so it handles cold-weather price jumps best.
- Exponential smoothing chose alpha = 0.95, close to "same as yesterday": day-to-day persistence is strong, which makes beating it meaningful.

## Sensitivities (training period, 2023 to 2025)

| Driver | $/MWh on-peak | HAC std error |
|---|---:|---:|
| +1 heating degree day | 1.07 | 0.20 |
| +1 cooling degree day | 4.54 | 0.68 |
| +$1/MMBtu Henry Hub | 11.56 | 1.99 |

- Heat moves PJM power about **4x more per degree than cold**: air conditioning runs on electricity, while much winter heating burns gas directly.
- $11.56/MWh per $1 of gas implies a marginal heat rate near 11.6 MMBtu/MWh.

## Finding: 2026 looks structurally different

| Year | Avg on-peak price | Avg Henry Hub | Implied heat rate | $/MWh per HDD | $/MWh per CDD | Price std dev |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2023 | $39.24 | $2.54 | 15.6 | 0.38 | 1.74 | $13.73 |
| 2024 | $40.91 | $2.23 | 19.2 | 0.36 | 2.56 | $19.44 |
| 2025 | $60.30 | $3.54 | 17.0 | 0.52 | 4.02 | $32.08 |
| 2026 | $90.19 | $3.41 | 26.2 | 1.06 | 7.32 | $96.54 |

Average price rose 50% from 2025 to 2026 while Henry Hub fell slightly. Weather sensitivity roughly doubled, and the implied heat rate rose from 17.0 to 26.2. This explains why regressions trained on 2023 to 2025 underpredicted 2026 by $23 to $32/MWh on average.

The data shows the effect; the causes are hypotheses still to test (coal retirements, load growth, regional gas basis). Part of the heat-rate increase may reflect regional gas prices in PJM running above Henry Hub in winter.

## Largest misses, and what they say

**Validation:** the computed Jan. 27, 2026 on-peak price of $890.00/MWh matches the published record of $890.01/MWh for that day ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/electric-power/012726-us-wholesale-power-prices-reach-record-highs-in-winter-storm-retail-electricity-outages-continue)), confirming the on-peak block construction.

The 15 largest misses fall into three groups (full list: `outliers.csv`):

1. **Extreme weather.** Winter Storm Fern (Jan. 27 to 28, 2026, $565 to $890/MWh) and July 2026 heat (84 to 88°F, $329 to $479/MWh). Scarcity pricing beyond what the 2023 to 2025 relationship had seen.
2. **Mild-weather spikes, fall 2026.** Sept. 17, Sept. 30, Oct. 1, Oct. 13 to 14: 67 to 75°F with almost no heating or cooling demand, yet $166 to $296/MWh vs. fitted $60 to $83. Weather can't explain these; likely candidates are maintenance-season outages or congestion.
3. **Overpredictions in earlier years.** Jan. 2024, when a $13.20 Henry Hub spike pushed the linear gas term far above where power cleared. Gas pass-through is not constant.

9 of the 15 largest misses are in 2026.

## Method

- **Y:** daily on-peak day-ahead price at Western Hub ($/MWh): weekdays, hours ending 8 to 23 Eastern, NERC holidays excluded, built from hourly LMPs.
- **Data type:** daily time series, Jan. 2023 to Nov. 2026.
- **Drivers:** average heating/cooling degree days (base 65°F) across 8 PJM demand centers (Philadelphia, Newark, Baltimore, Washington, Richmond, Pittsburgh, Columbus, Chicago); Henry Hub spot from the previous trading day (known before the day-ahead market clears); day-of-week and month fixed effects.
- **Split:** chronological. Train 2023 to 2025, test 2026. No random shuffling.
- **Models compared (15):** naive previous day, naive same day last year, moving averages (2, 5, 10, 20, 60 days), weighted moving averages (5, 20), exponential smoothing (alpha tuned on training data only), three fixed-effects regressions, a heat-rate model (price / gas, then multiplied back), and SARIMAX with weather and gas inputs.
- **Standard errors:** HAC (Newey-West) to account for autocorrelation in daily data.

## Data sources

- Hourly day-ahead and real-time LMPs: [Grid Status API](https://www.gridstatus.io) (`pjm_lmp_day_ahead_hourly`, `pjm_lmp_real_time_hourly`, location `WESTERN HUB`)
- Daily mean temperature: [Open-Meteo historical weather API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api)
- Henry Hub daily spot: [EIA](https://www.eia.gov/dnav/ng/hist/rngwhhdD.htm)

Raw data files are not committed; the notebook pulls and caches them.

## Limitations

- **Perfect weather foresight:** the backtest uses observed temperatures for each delivery day. Real forecasts would add error.
- **Henry Hub, not regional gas:** PJM's marginal gas units buy at regional hubs (e.g. TETCO M3, Transco Zone 6), which can trade far above Henry Hub in winter. No free daily source exists for those.
- **Missing drivers:** no generation outages, transmission congestion, or load forecast yet. The fall 2026 misses point to these.
- **Fixed relationships:** one set of coefficients estimated on 2023 to 2025; 2026 shows those relationships shifted.

## Next steps

## How to run

1. Open `pjm_westhub_model.ipynb` in Google Colab.
2. Add a Grid Status API key as a Colab secret named `GRIDSTATUS_API_KEY` (free tier is sufficient).
3. Runtime > Run all. The first run pulls about 4 years of data; later runs use the cache.

## Tools and workflow

Python, pandas, statsmodels, matplotlib, Grid Status API. I defined the question, the target variable, the on-peak rules, the data choices, and the model comparison, and interpreted the results; I used Claude (AI) to write and debug the code, and checked outputs against published market data.

This is a fundamentals and forecasting model, not a trading strategy.
